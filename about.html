About Botswana

The Land of Peace, Diamonds & Natural Wonder

Discover Botswana

Botswana is a peaceful and culturally rich country located in Southern Africa. Known for its political stability, breathtaking wildlife, strong traditions and rapidly growing economy, Botswana stands as one of Africa’s greatest success stories.

From the crystal waterways of the Okavango Delta to the endless landscapes of the Kalahari Desert, Botswana combines natural beauty with deep cultural heritage. The country is respected internationally for wildlife conservation, democratic leadership, and economic growth driven by diamonds, tourism and livestock.

Geography & Location

Botswana is a landlocked country bordered by Namibia, Zimbabwe, Zambia and South Africa. Much of the country is covered by the Kalahari Desert, giving Botswana its unique dry climate and vast open landscapes.

Despite its dry environment, Botswana is home to one of the world’s most remarkable natural wonders — the Okavango Delta. This inland delta transforms into a lush paradise filled with wildlife, waterways and wetlands.

Botswana is internationally recognised for protecting its environment and wildlife through conservation-focused tourism.

The Okavango Delta

The Okavango Delta is one of Botswana’s greatest treasures and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Every year, floodwaters from Angola flow into Botswana, creating a rich ecosystem full of elephants, lions, hippos, crocodiles and hundreds of bird species.

The Delta is one of the most famous safari destinations in the world and plays a major role in Botswana’s tourism industry.

Traditional mokoro canoe rides through the waterways provide visitors with a unique experience deeply connected to Botswana’s natural heritage.

Diamonds & Economic Growth

Botswana is one of the world’s leading diamond producers. Since diamonds were discovered shortly after independence, the industry has transformed the country’s economy.

Revenue from diamonds has helped Botswana invest in education, healthcare, roads and national development. Unlike many countries rich in natural resources, Botswana is often praised for managing its diamond wealth responsibly.

The diamond industry continues to play an important role in Botswana’s economy and global identity.

Beef & Livestock Industry

Cattle farming is deeply connected to Botswana’s culture, economy and daily life. For many families, cattle represent wealth, status and tradition.

Botswana’s beef industry is internationally respected for its quality and exports beef to markets around the world.

Livestock farming has supported rural communities for generations and remains one of the country’s most important industries.

History & The Three Dikgosi

Botswana was formerly known as the Bechuanaland Protectorate during British colonial rule. Before independence, traditional leaders played a major role in protecting the country’s future.

In 1895, three important dikgosi — Khama III, Sebele I and Bathoen I — travelled to Britain to protect Bechuanaland from external control.

Their leadership helped preserve Botswana’s land and identity, and they are remembered today as national heroes.

Botswana officially gained independence on 30 September 1966 under its first president, Sir Seretse Khama.

Culture & Traditions

Botswana is home to diverse ethnic groups and tribes, including the Bangwato, Bakwena, Batawana and Bakgatla.

Respect, community and tradition are central values in Botswana society. Traditional ceremonies, storytelling, music and dance remain important parts of everyday life.

Traditional foods such as seswaa, bogobe and dikgobe reflect the country’s strong cultural identity.
