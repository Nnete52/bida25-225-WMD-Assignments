Culture of Botswana

Traditions, Pride, Community & Identity

A Living Heritage

The culture of Botswana is built on strong values of respect, kindness, family unity and community responsibility. Culture is not only seen during celebrations, but also in the way people greet, speak, care for elders and support one another.

Although Botswana is modern and developing, many traditions remain deeply valued. Language, storytelling, music, traditional food, ceremonies and moral teachings continue to connect generations.

One important cultural principle is Botho, which represents humanity, humility, compassion and treating others with dignity.

Tribes & Communities of Botswana

Bakwena

One of the historic Tswana groups. Their totem is the crocodile, and they played an important role in early Botswana history.

Bamangwato

Associated with the central region and the legacy of Sir Seretse Khama. They are one of Botswana’s well-known communities.

Batawana

Strongly connected to the Okavango region. Their identity is tied to waterways, wildlife and northern Botswana heritage.

Bakgatla

A respected community in the Kgatleng region with traditions shared across Botswana and neighbouring South Africa.

Botswana is also home to many other communities such as Bangwaketse, Balete, Barolong, Batlokwa, Kalanga, Basarwa and more. Each group contributes language, customs, food and traditions to the nation.

Traditional Clothing & Identity

Traditional clothing is often worn during weddings, initiation ceremonies, national celebrations and cultural festivals.

Colourful fabrics, shawls, headwraps and beadwork are used to express beauty, dignity and belonging. Clothing often reflects respect for the occasion.

Music, Dance & Celebration

Dance is one of the most expressive parts of Botswana culture. It is used during celebrations, weddings, festivals and community gatherings.

Popular styles include Setapa, known for energetic footwork, and Tsutsube, often linked to rhythm, movement and storytelling.

Through dance, people celebrate joy, identity and unity.

Folklore & Oral Tradition

Before books were common, wisdom was shared through storytelling. Elders told stories at night to entertain children and teach lessons.

Many folktales featured clever hares, proud lions, greedy hyenas or foolish characters who learned consequences from their actions.

Hare stories often praised intelligence and quick thinking, while hyena stories warned against greed and selfishness.

These stories helped preserve morals, humour and community memory.

Traditional Flavours of Botswana

Food plays an important role in family gatherings, ceremonies and hospitality. Meals are often shared communally.

Seswaa
Slow-cooked pounded meat often served during celebrations.

Bogobe
A traditional porridge made from sorghum or maize meal.

Dikgobe
A nutritious mixture of beans and grains enjoyed by many families.
